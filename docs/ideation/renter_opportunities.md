# Reality Check — renter opportunities, from first principles

Forward-looking note on where the shipped checker could go next.

Written 2026-11-14. Research and thinking only; nothing was built or changed.

How to read the evidence marks: **[R]** = the page was opened and read; **[S]** = seen in search results only; **[T]** = endpoint was called today and returned real data; **[C]** = seen in our own cache or kit docs. Reddit blocks automated reading, so Reddit views come from articles that summarise threads. Hours, and the "minutes it takes by hand" figures, are my estimates.

---

## 0. The one-paragraph answer

The current product checks **what the listing says** (beds, size, floors, year). Renters' regrets come from **what the listing does not say**: the real monthly cost, noise, condition, size that does not work for their furniture, parking, light. The decision is also **personal** (my bed, my car, my dog, my commute) and **comparative** (this place versus the other one on my shortlist). So the opportunity is to turn Reality Check from "claims versus records" into **"the tour and the homework, done before you go"**: five questions answered with evidence the renter can touch — *Is it real? What will it truly cost? Will my life fit in it? What is it like to live there (noise, light, what is outside)? What happens later (rent rises, flood, building record)?* The sponsor's data is the unfair advantage in each: CoStar's rents and building records for cost and fairness, the Matterport scan for fit and condition, the aerial for orientation, sun, noise sources and neighbours.

## Part 1 — What a renter needs to know, and how they find it today

### 1.1 The decision

"Should I tour / apply / sign for **this** place, at **this** price, instead of the others I am looking at?" Behind it sit five fears:

1. **Being cheated** — the listing is not what it claims, or is not a real offer.
2. **Paying more than I think** — fees, utilities, parking, pet rent, a concession that vanishes at renewal.
3. **It does not fit my life** — my furniture, my car, my commute, my work-from-home desk.
4. **It is unpleasant to live in** — noise, dark rooms, pests, bad management, a view of a dumpster.
5. **A nasty surprise later** — big rent rise, flood, building sold, construction next door.

**Fair-housing note on safety (#11).** Realtor.com and Trulia removed crime maps in 2021 over redlining concerns (https://therealdeal.com/2021/12/15/realtor-com-nixes-crime-map-in-effort-to-promote-fair-housing [S]). A HUD letter of April 2026 says sharing crime or school data equally is not steering by itself (https://www.housingwire.com/articles/hud-says-agents-can-discuss-crime-and-school-data/ [R]), but that guidance can be reversed and state law still applies. Recommendation: no neighbourhood safety scores, no crime heat maps, no demographics. Offer only building-level, objective facts shown the same way to everyone, and a link to the official police data for those who want it.

## Part 3 — Opportunities

A principle for the owner's two stages: **every Stage 1 widget is a thin screen over one pure function** — inputs in, evidence out. The renter drives it first. When their use confirms it is wanted and right, the *same function* is registered as a tool in the harness and the agent calls it on every check. What the renter did by hand (which comps they kept, how they rotated the floor plan, which side their unit is on) is saved as calibration and as test cases for the agent.

Scores: wow / renter value / sponsor showcase, each 1–5.

### 1. True monthly cost — "what will I really pay?"

- **Need / today:** "The rent said $1,450; I pay $1,700." (illustrative) Top regret; fees hidden deep in leases (FTC v. Greystar). Today: hunt for a fee sheet or phone the office.
- **Idea:** rebuild the price from every source and show the all-in monthly figure and first-year total, with each line marked *listed*, *from CoStar's record*, *estimated*, or *ask*.
- **Stage 1:** a cost builder the renter drives: toggles for "I have a car / two cars", "one dog", "lease length", "concession on offer". Each line shows its source; unknown lines become a question for the leasing office. A second number shows **renewal rent** when a concession is in play.
- **Stage 2:** the agent reads the listing page and the community's own site (`propertyWebsiteUrl`) for fee wording, fills the builder, and writes the "questions to ask" list.
- **Data:** base rent from the page; CoStar parking fees, pet rent/deposit/fees, metering type, lease terms — **verified in docs**. Electricity estimate from unit size × state price (EIA) — **verified**. Mandatory fees (trash, package, technology) come only from the page or the community site — **likely, varies**.
- **Approach:** a fixed schema of cost lines; extraction by the agent into that schema with a quoted snippet per line; deterministic arithmetic; net-effective formula for concessions; nothing invented — an empty line stays "ask".
- **Effort:** 5 h. **Scores:** 3 / 5 / 4.
- **Risks:** CoStar policies are "as of last research"; say so. Utility figure is an estimate with a range. Never say fees are "hidden" — say "not shown on this page".

### 2. Fair rent and bargaining position — "is this price fair, can I negotiate?"

- **Need / today:** Rentometer and friends, built on asking rents with wide error.
- **Idea:** an appraiser-style comparison using CoStar's own snapshot: same-bedroom floor plans in nearby communities of similar age and rating, in $ per square foot, plus how many units this community has sitting empty.
- **Stage 1:** a "pick your comparables" panel: a map of nearby communities, sliders for distance, age and rating; the renter ticks which ones count and watches the position of this rent move along a line. Beside it, a plain statement: "N of M units are available here; similar communities nearby have X%".
- **Stage 2:** the agent chooses comparables, explains why each was chosen, and states the bargaining position in one sentence.
- **Data:** apartment-listing rents, sizes, `numUnitsAvailable`, `totalNumUnits`; property `buildingRating`, year built, `vacancyPercent` — **verified in docs**. Zillow ZIP index as a backdrop — **verified**.
- **Approach:** distance filter on coordinates, match beds and style, drop zero rents, median and range of $ per sq ft, show count used; availability share compared with the comparable set.
- **Effort:** 4 h. **Scores:** 3 / 5 / 5 (this is CoStar doing what CoStar is known for, pointed at a renter).
- **Risks:** asking rents, one date; our sample is the scanned buildings, not the whole market — state both. Compare within a city only.

### 3. Sun, light and orientation — "does it get light, which way does it face?"

- **Need / today:** compass app during the tour, visiting at different times. Nobody shows it on a listing.
- **Idea:** place the scanned unit onto the aerial image, then compute direct-sun hours for each room through the year, including shade from neighbouring buildings.
- **Stage 1 (naturally human-driven):** the renter sees the scan's floor-plan outline floating over the top-down aerial of the building and **drags and rotates it** to where the unit sits, picks the floor. Then a time-of-day and season slider shows which rooms get sun; the 3D viewer turns to face the lit window.
- **Stage 2:** the agent aligns automatically — matches wall directions to the building footprint, uses the unit's floor number, finds windows by looking at panoramas — and reports "bedroom faces west: afternoon sun, hot in a Houston summer; living room gets no direct sun November–January".
- **Data:** scan room outline — bounding boxes from the viewer SDK (**documented**), true outlines **unknown**; aerial top-down and elevation raster — **verified in cache**; neighbouring heights from the raster or Overture — **verified**; sun maths — **verified**. Scan compass heading — **does not exist**, hence the manual alignment.
- **Approach:** user alignment gives a rotation and position; window bearings from the panorama the vision model marks as containing a window plus camera direction; for each hour, cast a ray toward the sun over the elevation raster to test for shade; sum hours per season.
- **Effort:** 9–12 h. **Scores:** 5 / 4 / 5 (scan + aerial + elevation fused; no listing site does this).
- **Risks:** wrong alignment gives a wrong answer — show the alignment and let the renter fix it; say "estimated". Which unit in a large community the scan represents may be unknown: say "a unit of this layout placed here".

### 4. What is outside, and how loud — "the noise forecast"

- **Need / today:** #2 regret. Advice is to visit on a Saturday night. Few do.
- **Idea:** a map of every noise source around the building, the measured decibel level, and which side of the building each one hits — including the business **under** the apartments, which only CoStar knows.
- **Stage 1:** a noise layer on the aerial with toggles (roads, trains, flight path, nightlife, sirens, businesses in the building) and a day/night switch; the renter marks their unit's side and floor and gets "what you would hear here".
- **Stage 2:** the agent writes it up, for example (illustrative figures): "The bedroom side faces a road carrying 48,000 vehicles a day at 60 m. A rail crossing 400 m away has 9 trains at night and horns are sounded. A bar and a gym occupy the ground floor."
- **Data:** national noise raster — **verified** (needs tile decoding; fallback is the legend band); traffic counts, rail crossings — **verified**; LA airport contours — **verified**; venues from OpenStreetMap/Overture — **verified, flaky**; ground-floor tenants from CoStar occupancy + company industry — **verified in docs** (coverage per building varies); LA 311 noise requests — **verified**.
- **Approach:** radius queries cached per building; bearing from building centre to each source to assign a façade; simple distance falloff for a "likely audible" label rather than a made-up score; the oblique aerial from that direction as proof.
- **Effort:** 6 h. **Scores:** 4 / 5 / 4.
- **Risks:** we cannot hear neighbours through walls — say what this covers and what it does not. Present as sources and distances, not a rating of the neighbourhood. Noise raster is from 2020.

### 5. Will my stuff fit — the tape measure in the scan

- **Need / today:** tape measure at the viewing; listings rarely give room dimensions; phone measuring apps are unreliable (Brick Underground).
- **Idea:** real room sizes from the scan, and the renter's own furniture tested against them.
- **Stage 1:** "My things" list (king bed, 84-inch sofa, desk, with standard sizes pre-filled and editable). The renter clicks a room; we show its measured area and dimensions; they click two points in the tour to measure anything (doorway, alcove, closet depth); in floor-plan view they drag a furniture footprint into the room.
- **Stage 2:** the agent reports per room: "Bedroom 2 takes a queen with 28 inches each side; a king leaves 9 inches." It also gives **the breakdown of the square feet** — how much of the advertised size is bedroom, living space, closets and hallway — and $ per usable square foot.
- **Data:** room areas — **verified in cache**; room width/depth — SDK bounding boxes **documented**, exact dimensions **unknown**; point-to-point measuring through the SDK's built-in tool and cursor 3D position — **documented**; placing 3D furniture — needs SDK Bundle, **unknown**.
- **Approach:** start with the built-in measure tool and room boxes (no extra access needed); furniture as 2D footprints on the floor-plan view; fit rule = footprint inside the room box with a clearance; summed room area versus advertised size.
- **Effort:** 5 h for measuring + footprints + breakdown; +4 h for 3D furniture. **Scores:** 5 / 4 / 5.
- **Risks:** bounding boxes overstate odd-shaped rooms — label as approximate. A scan may be of a different unit of the same plan.

### 6. Is this the unit I would get? — photo-to-scan match

- **Need / today:** bait-and-switch is the most reported listing problem; renters ask to see the actual unit and are sometimes refused.
- **Idea:** match each listing photo to a spot in the 3D scan; say which photos are of this layout and which could not be found.
- **Stage 1:** the renter clicks any photo on the listing page and chooses "show me this spot in 3D"; the viewer flies to the matching panorama, side by side. Photos with no match are labelled "not found in this scan — may be another unit, a model, or a shared area".
- **Stage 2:** the agent matches all photos up front, notes photo age (`originationDate`) against renovation dates, and flags likely digital staging in neutral words.
- **Data:** panorama images and positions — **verified**; listing photos from the page — **verified**; CoStar photo dates — field exists, fill **unknown**.
- **Approach:** describe each panorama face once with the vision model (room type, fixtures, layout) and cache; for a clicked photo, shortlist by room type then let the vision model pick the best view and direction; move the viewer there.
- **Effort:** 6–8 h. **Scores:** 5 / 4 / 5.
- **Risks:** false "no match". Wording must stay neutral: "we could not find this view", never "fake". Staging is legal.

### 7. The agent tours it for you — ask the scan anything

- **Need / today:** generic tour checklists; the items that can be seen (washer/dryer, dishwasher, AC type, outlets, closets, windows per room, gas or electric stove, flooring) still need a visit.
- **Idea:** the agent walks every panorama, answers the checklist items it can see, takes you to the evidence, and hands you a short list of what only a visit can answer.
- **Stage 1:** a question box and a set of checklist chips. The renter asks "is there a washer in the unit?" — the viewer flies there and tags it. Each answer is "seen / not seen in the scan / cannot tell".
- **Stage 2:** the full pre-walk runs on every check and produces a **tour plan for this unit**: "Seen: washer and dryer, central air vents, 2 closets. Not visible: outlets in bedroom 2. Check in person: water pressure, phone signal, smells, the trash area."
- **Data:** panoramas and positions — **verified**; room tags — **partly** (vision fills the gaps).
- **Approach:** one vision pass per panorama into a fixed fact sheet with the panorama id and direction; answers are lookups into that sheet, so they are fast and repeatable; client-side tags in the viewer mark the spot.
- **Effort:** 5 h. **Scores:** 4 / 4 / 5.
- **Risks:** "not seen" is not "absent". Condition judgements (stains, wear) should be shown, not scored.

### 8. What happens later — rent rises, hazards, building changes

- **Need / today:** almost nobody checks; the surprises are large. LA renters often do not know whether a unit is rent-stabilised; Texas renters rely on a landlord notice for flood.
- **Idea:** one "later" card per city, built from records.
  - **Los Angeles:** rent-stabilised or not, and therefore the most the rent can rise next year (3% under the city ordinance for July 2026–June 2027; 8.7% under the state cap; no cap if exempt); just-cause protection; earthquake retrofit status for soft-story buildings, shown next to the oblique aerial where the open parking level is visible; fault and liquefaction zones.
  - **Houston:** flood zone, Harvey high-water marks nearby, draft new floodplain, ground elevation, **and the floor the unit is on**; a reminder that Texas has no rent cap and that the landlord must give a written flood notice.
  - **Both:** whether the building was sold recently and under what conditions, and its renovation history (CoStar).
- **Stage 1:** the renter asks "can they raise my rent?", "has this flooded?", "is it retrofitted?" from chips; each answer shows the record and the date.
- **Stage 2:** automatic on every check, with the questions to put to the landlord.
- **Data:** ZIMAS parcel report, soft-story dataset, FEMA, Harris County flood layers, USGS elevation — **verified today**; CoStar year built, sale comps, renovations — **verified in docs**; rent-cap percentages — from city and county bulletins (confirm before launch).
- **Approach:** point → parcel id → cached record pulls; a small rule table for which rent law applies (city boundary, year built, stabilisation flag); address join for retrofit status.
- **Effort:** 4 h for one city, 6 h for both. **Scores:** 3 / 5 / 3.
- **Risks:** this is legal information, not advice — say so and link the official page. The stabilisation flag covers "at least one unit". Flood zone is not a prediction.

### 9. Is this a real offer? — identity check on any website

- **Need / today:** search the address for other listings, compare prices, check who owns it. Misleading offers mostly start on Facebook and Craigslist, and copy real listings.
- **Idea:** recognise the address as a known CoStar community and compare the page with the community's own listing.
- **Stage 1:** a small strip on the page, for example (illustrative figures): "This address is an N-unit community managed by [company]. Its own listing asks $1,450–$1,900 for one-bedrooms. This page asks $900. Official site: [link]." One button: "contact the leasing office directly".
- **Stage 2:** the agent adds a check for payment-pressure wording on the page and for the same photos or address at different prices elsewhere.
- **Data:** CoStar apartment listing, management company name, `propertyWebsiteUrl` — **verified in docs** (fill of the URL **unknown**).
- **Approach:** address match (already possible), rent compared to the same-bedroom range, a short list of pressure phrases from FTC guidance.
- **Effort:** 2–3 h. **Scores:** 3 / 5 / 5.
- **Risks:** wording — "does not match the community's own listing", never "scam". Sub-lets and lease takeovers are legitimately cheaper. Company names only; never a person.

### 10. Building record — "is this place well run?"

- **Need / today:** reviews (which can be gamed) and, in a few cities, violation lookups.
- **Idea:** a dated timeline for the building from official records, plus what CoStar knows about how it is run.
- **Stage 1:** a timeline the renter can filter: inspections and violations, code cases, permits (what was actually repaired or renovated and when), sale, renovation. Beside it: who manages it and how many communities they manage in our data, whether there is an on-site office, vacancy compared with similar buildings.
- **Stage 2:** the agent summarises patterns ("three open code cases in 2026; roof and HVAC renovated 2022") and adds questions for the tour.
- **Data:** **LA: verified today and fresh.** **Houston: mostly unavailable** — permits by spreadsheet only. CoStar management company, on-site office, vacancy, renovations — **verified in docs**.
- **Approach:** parcel id join, counts by year and type, plain statements with links to the official record.
- **Effort:** 4 h (LA only). **Scores:** 3 / 4 / 3.
- **Risks:** highest of all ideas for wrong or unfair impressions. Report counts and dates, not judgements. Eviction-notice data: counts per building at most, never names or unit numbers; consider leaving it out. No private landlord names.

### 11. Cheap, high-signal extras

| Idea | Stage 1 | Data | Effort | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Questions for the leasing office** — every "unknown" in the report becomes a ready-to-send question | "Copy my questions" button | Our own gaps | 1 h | Turns honesty about gaps into value |

## Part 4 — Recommendation

### 4.1 What the current "claims versus sources" framing gets wrong

1. **It verifies what was said; regrets come from what was not said.** Year built and floor count rarely change a renter's mind. Cost, noise, light, fit and the unit actually on offer do.
2. **It is binary and sounds like an accusation.** "Mismatch" invites a fight about who is right. Renters want "so what, and what do I do next" — a figure, a view, a question to ask.
3. **It is not personal.** The same report for everyone ignores that the answer depends on my bed, my car, my dog, my hours.
4. **It stops at the record.** The renter's own checking ends on a tour; the product should end in a tour plan and a list of questions.
5. **It treats the scan and the aerial as proof for a claim.** They are far more valuable as instruments — a tape measure, a compass, a window to look out of.
6. **It ignores the comparison.** People choose between two or three places.

Keep the claim checks, but as one quiet section ("the listing's facts check out / two differ"), not the headline.

### 4.2 Build order for about 18 hours

| Order | What | Hours | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reframe the report around the five questions; add **questions for the leasing office** | 2 | Changes the story at once; every later piece slots in |
| 2 | **True monthly cost** (idea 1) | 5 | Top regret; CoStar fields ready; easy to get right |
| 3 | **Fair rent and bargaining position** (idea 2) | 4 | Deepest use of the sponsor's core data; low risk |
| 4 | **Will my stuff fit** — measure tool, room sizes, footprints, square-foot breakdown (idea 5) | 5 | Best hands-on moment; uses only access we have verified |
| 5 | **Ask the scan / tour plan** (idea 7) | 5 | Makes the agent visibly do work; feeds idea 6 |
| 6 | **Sun and orientation** with manual alignment (idea 3) | 9–12 | The headline of the demo; start early in parallel, one owner |
| 7 | **Noise sources** (idea 4) — reuse the alignment for "your side" | 6 | #2 regret; CoStar ground-floor tenants are a unique touch |
| 8 | **Later card** for LA rent law + retrofit, Houston flood (idea 8) | 4–6 | High value, pure lookups, good for the LA sample |
| 9 | **Identity check** strip (idea 9) | 2–3 | Tiny; shows "works on any website" has a point |
| 10 | Photo-to-scan match (idea 6), shortlist compare | 6–8, 3 | Only if time remains |

With several builders in parallel, items 2–5 and 6 can run side by side with separate file ownership; 7 depends on 6's alignment.

### 4.3 The two-minute demo

All figures in this script are illustrative.

1. A listing on an ordinary site. Click. The strip says what this address is and that the page's price sits inside the community's own range (identity).
2. "It says $1,450. You would pay **$1,742**." Toggle the dog and the car; the number moves; three lines say "ask" (true cost).
3. "Fair? Here are nine similar one-bedrooms within a mile from CoStar's records — this one is X% above the middle, and Y% of its units are empty: there is room to ask." (fair rent)
4. Into the 3D tour. Drop a king bed in the bedroom: it does not clear the door. Measure the alcove with two clicks (fit).
5. Slide the floor plan onto the aerial. Drag the time slider: the bedroom takes the afternoon sun; the oblique view shows the six-lane road on that side and a bar on the ground floor (sun + noise).
6. LA unit: "Rent-stabilised: the most it can rise next year is 3%. Retrofit: complete." Houston unit: "Zone X; Harvey high-water mark 300 m away, 4 ft; this unit is on floor 3."
7. Close on the tour plan: four things the agent already checked in the scan, three to check in person, five questions ready to send.

### 4.4 Cheap but high-signal

Questions for the leasing office (1 h), identity strip (2–3 h), parking card (2 h), square-foot breakdown from room areas we already hold (1–2 h), LA rent-law line (2 h), "open my tabs" (1 h).

### 4.5 What to skip, and why

| Skip | Why |
|---|---|
| Crime maps, safety scores, demographics, school ratings | Fair-housing risk; data is unreliable at building level |
| Ingesting Google / Yelp / ApartmentRatings reviews | No API; scraping breaks terms and bot protection. Link out instead |
| Cell signal and broadband | Sources need keys or are blocked; carrier maps do not show indoor signal anyway |
| Door-to-door transit time | No free keyless router; not worth standing up our own for now |
| Houston building conduct record | Data is stale or absent; do LA only and say so |
| Per-building eviction court counts in Houston | Free, but contains tenant names and needs careful handling — not a hackathon job |
| Counting parking spaces or cars from the aerial with computer vision | Fragile; CoStar already has spaces and ratios |
| Neighbour-through-the-wall noise, pests, water pressure | Cannot be known remotely; put them on the in-person list honestly |
| A single overall "score" for the listing | Hides the reasoning and invites unfair conclusions |

## Part 5 — What could not be verified

- **Matterport:** whether our account returns room width/depth, wall outlines, door openings and ceiling height (enterprise extension); whether we have the SDK Bundle for inserting 3D furniture; SDK key requirements. Only room areas, tags and panorama positions are confirmed from our cache.
- **Aerial:** whether older captures and post-disaster imagery are reachable with our key; whether ready-made attributes are licensed. The public API reference is behind a login.
- **CoStar kit:** fill rates for unit `floorNumber`, `propertyWebsiteUrl` and photo `originationDate`; how many apartment buildings with unit scans also have ground-floor tenants. Amenity names are missing from the kit.
- **Renter evidence:** no first-hand Reddit quotes (Reddit refused automated reading); no systematic data on how often square footage is overstated; minutes-per-manual-check are my estimates.
- **Law:** rent-cap percentages and state fee-disclosure laws were taken from agency bulletins and secondary summaries, not statute text; confirm before showing them as fact. Whether California's fee law covers long-term rentals is unclear.
- **Public data:** the Harris County eviction extract and the large appraisal-district files were confirmed to exist but not pulled; LA County assessor unit fields were tested only on non-apartment parcels; the national noise raster needs our own tile decoding and is from 2020; OpenStreetMap's query service was unreliable today.
